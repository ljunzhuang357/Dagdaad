# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Dutch speakers who want to notice and record everyday kindness. Not productivity-obsessed, not religious, not chasing self-improvement metrics — just people who want to see their own trail of small good deeds over time. Uses the app briefly each day (1-2 minutes), often on mobile in a quiet moment.

## Product Purpose

A one-good-deed-a-day journal. Write down one nice thing you did for someone else each day. See your calendar fill up with colour over the month. No streaks, no badges, no social comparison — just the quiet satisfaction of noticing your own kindness.

## Brand Personality

warm, humble, daily

Like a cosy Dutch café — friendly without being loud, genuine without being sentimental. Speaks plainly, doesn't try to impress. The app equivalent of a handwritten note, not a marketing email.

## Anti-references

- Gamified habit trackers (streaks, badges, leaderboards, "don't break the chain")
- Social media feeds or sharing features
- Religious or self-help preaching tones
- Cold/minimalist corporate dashboards
- Over-designed "wellness" apps with heavy animations and illustrations

## Design Principles

1. **Small is enough** — The design never makes the user feel guilty. One entry is always enough. Visual emphasis on completion and presence, not quantity.
2. **Warmth without sentimentality** — Cosy colours and rounded corners, but no cutesy illustrations, no excessive animation. Warmth in material, not in decoration.
3. **Daily rhythm, not streaks** — The calendar fills up over time naturally. No "you missed a day" messaging. The empty today is an invitation, not a failure.
4. **Touch-friendly, low-effort** — Mobile-first interaction, large tap targets, minimal typing. The act of recording should take less effort than the act of kindness itself.
5. **Dutch directness** — No marketing fluff, no onboarding tour. Show the interface, let the user figure it out. Trust them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA minimum
- Touch targets ≥44px
- No essential information conveyed through colour alone
- Reduced motion respected via `prefers-reduced-motion`
